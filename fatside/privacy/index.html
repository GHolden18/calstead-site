Privacy policy

Fatside, by Calstead Studios. Last updated November 20, 2026.

Fatside is made by Calstead Studios. Your golf stays on your phone: there’s no account, no ads and no tracking, and we never sell your data.

What stays on your phone

Everything you do in Fatside is kept in the app’s storage on your phone: your picks and scores, streak, decision log and patterns, golfer type, your bag and carries, saved courses, coins, energy, looks and settings. We don’t have a copy and can’t see it. The home screen widget reads your streak and today’s score from the same place.

What leaves your phone

A few things need the internet. Each sends only what it needs:

  • Course search. The words you type go to OpenGolfAPI (opengolfapi.org) to find matching courses.
  • Hole maps. The first time you open a real course, the app asks our server (Supabase) for that course’s maps from OpenStreetMap, using the course’s location (not yours). If no one has opened the course before, the app gets the maps from a public OpenStreetMap server, the Overpass API, and sends a copy to our server for the next player. The maps are public data about the course, nothing about you. The app keeps them, so it asks once.
  • Feedback. If you send feedback, your note, the part of the app you picked, the app version and your phone’s model and system version go to our server (Supabase). Your name and anything that identifies you aren’t sent, unless you type them in the note.
  • Course codes. If you enter a code from a golf course, the code, that course, the time and the app version go to our server (Supabase), so we can tell the course how many golfers its code brought in. Your name and anything that identifies you aren’t sent.
  • Updates. The app checks Expo, the service that delivers Fatside’s updates, for a newer version. The check sends a random ID made for this install (not tied to you, an account or your phone’s hardware), the app’s version and platform, and a short error message if the app failed to start. Expo says it doesn’t use this for ads or tracking.
  • Like any website, each of these services sees your phone’s IP address when it answers.

What we don’t do

Fatside has no accounts and no analytics. It never asks for your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone, and it doesn’t use advertising IDs. We don’t sell your data or share it for advertising.

When you choose to share

A score card you share goes only where you send it from your phone’s share sheet. Links to sources open in your browser. If you test Fatside through TestFlight, Apple may collect crash reports and any feedback you send, under Apple’s own privacy policy.

Purchases

In this version, Fatside Pro, packs, energy and coins are samples and take no payment. When purchases go live, Apple will handle payment (we never see your card), and this policy will say what our purchase service receives before that happens.

Your data, your call

You › Export my data saves everything Fatside keeps as a file. You › Delete all my data erases it from the phone, and deleting the app does too. We keep no copy of your golf, so there’s nothing of it to delete on our side. Feedback notes are kept until we’ve read and dealt with them; to have one deleted, write to us with roughly when you sent it and what it said. For update checks, Expo keeps request records as its privacy policy describes and deletes its raw request logs after three days.

The services we use

OpenGolfAPI, OpenStreetMap’s Overpass API, Supabase and Expo get only what’s listed above. We only use services that protect that data at least as well as this policy does, and each has its own privacy policy.

Children

Fatside isn’t made for children under 13, and it doesn’t knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Changes

When this policy changes, we’ll update it here with a new date, and tell you in the app if the change is a big one.
